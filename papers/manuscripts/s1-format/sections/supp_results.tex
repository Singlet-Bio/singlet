\section{Detailed results}
\label{sec:sresults}

This section gives detail beyond the main text. The panel has \nm{nMatrices} count matrices from \nm{nDatasets} datasets
in \nm{nProtocolClasses} protocol classes, with \nm{fracOnesMinPct}--\nm{fracOnesMaxPct}\% of non-zeros equal to one
(Table~\ref{tab:datasets}); the formats are listed in Table~\ref{tab:formats} and every format's size and read rate in
Table~\ref{M-tab:main}. Timings use \nm{threadsHeadline} cores of a \nm{cpu} node (Section~\ref{sec:methods}). \sone{}
is reported as its reader returns a matrix by default: genes in their stored, frequency-sorted order, with the gene names
permuted to match, one file per matrix.

\subsection{Size and read speed per matrix}

\sone{} files take a median of \nm{s1BpnMedian} bits per non-zero (range \nm{s1BpnMin}--\nm{s1BpnMax}), gene identifiers
and barcodes included (Table~\ref{tab:size}). Paired by matrix, gzip AnnData is \nm{ratio-h5ad_gzip}$\times$ larger, and the
smallest other file on each matrix, \nm{bestOtherFormat} on \nm{bestOtherFormatCount} of them, is \nm{ratioBestOther}$\times$
larger. The two generic-codec controls show that the gain is in the representation, not the entropy coder: the raw CSC
arrays with zstd-19 take \nm{size-csc_zstd19} bits per non-zero, and Zarr v3 with \sone-sized chunks and Blosc zstd
\nm{size-zarr3}. Moving gene identifiers into a pack shared by every dataset of one annotation saves little
(\nm{size-s1_pack} bits per non-zero).

From a cold file \sone{} reads a median of \nm{dec-s1-cold-t4} million non-zeros per second, against \nm{dec-zarr-cold-t4}
for AnnData Zarr and \nm{dec-h5ad_gzip-cold-t4} for gzip AnnData (Table~\ref{tab:decode}). Paired against the fastest
other format on each matrix, it is faster on \nm{dec-s1-fastest-count} of \nm{dec-paired-n} matrices, by a median of
\nm{dec-speedup-each}$\times$ (range \nm{dec-speedup-each-lo}--\nm{dec-speedup-each-hi}); the exceptions are small
matrices, at most \nm{dec-s1-slower-max-mnnz} million non-zeros, where costs per file that do not grow with the matrix,
such as reading names, weigh more. Returning the source gene order costs a sort per cell:
\nm{dec-s1canonical-cold-t4} instead of \nm{dec-s1-cold-t4} million non-zeros per second. The codec alone, from memory,
runs at \nm{codec_cpp-native-avx2-t1} million non-zeros per second on one core with AVX2 and
\nm{codec_cpp-native-scalar-t1} with the portable scalar kernel that processors without AVX2, including ARM, use; all
kernels give identical output. The AVX-512 kernel needs VBMI2, which the benchmark CPU lacks, and was not measured.

Few formats gain from more cores (Fig.~\ref{M-fig:wow}d): only \nm{par-formats-n} of \nm{par-formats-of} read faster on
four cores than on one. \sone{} reads a cold file \nm{speedup-s1-t4} times faster on four cores than on one
(\nm{speedup-s1-t8} on eight), against \nm{speedup-soma-t4} for TileDB-SOMA and \nm{speedup-mtx_gz-t4} for MTX.gz. The
codec consumes \nm{scale-s1codec-gbit-t1}, \nm{scale-s1codec-gbit-t4} and \nm{scale-s1codec-gbit-t8}\,Gbit/s of stored
bits on one, four and eight cores, so from four cores on it outpaces a 1\,Gbit/s link. The uncompressed arrays are
limited by the disk, and gzip AnnData decodes on one thread whatever the number of cores.

\subsection{Network-bound reading per matrix}

A reader runs at the speed of the network when it consumes stored bits at least as fast as the link delivers them,
$D_p b \ge B$; it then delivers $B/b$ non-zeros per second (Eq.~\ref{M-eq:reff}). On \nm{threadsHeadline} cores the
\sone{} reader consumes \nm{net-knee-s1}\,Gbit/s of stored bits, and the codec alone
\nm{net-knee-s1-per-core}\,Gbit/s per core (Fig.~\ref{fig:snet}a). The fastest other format under 12 bits per non-zero, \nm{net-knee-compact-best-name}, consumes
\nm{net-knee-compact-best}\,Gbit/s and cannot be read in part. Per matrix, \sone{} delivers non-zeros faster than the
uncompressed arrays on \nm{wire-wins-1} of \nm{wire-n-1} matrices at 1\,Gbit/s and \nm{wire-wins-10} of \nm{wire-n-10}
at 10\,Gbit/s (median gains \nm{wire-gain-1}$\times$ and \nm{wire-gain-10}$\times$). The uncompressed arrays catch up
only above a median of \nm{wire-crossover-gbit}\,Gbit/s, and on \nm{wire-crossover-never} of \nm{wire-crossover-n}
matrices never, because there \sone{} decodes faster even from memory.

\subsection{Minibatches by storage tier}

From \nm{fig1b-dataset} through the emulated object store, the \sone{} reader delivers \nm{thr-s1_stream-s3_0.1g},
\nm{thr-s1_stream-s3_1g} and \nm{thr-s1_stream-s3_10g} cells per second at 0.1, 1 and 10\,Gbit/s, filling
\nm{linkuse-s3_0.1g}\% and \nm{linkuse-s3_1g}\% of the two slower links. It gains nothing at 10\,Gbit/s because decoding
then limits it: a block of 64 cells costs the decoding of the whole chunks around it, which keeps the reader below the
rate at which the whole matrix decodes from memory (\nm{net-ds-decoder-cells} cells per second). From the node's spinning
disk, where every block is a seek, it delivers \nm{thr-s1_stream-local} cells per second, and from the caching shared
filesystem \nm{thr-s1_stream-nfs} (Table~\ref{tab:minibatch}). The first minibatch is ready after \nm{ttfm-s1}\,s, against
\nm{ttfm-best-other}\,s for the fastest alternative (\nm{ttfm-best-other-name}).

\subsection{Latency, round trips and requests in flight}

On a 1\,Gbit/s link with 30\,ms latency the first minibatch arrives in a median of \nm{ttfm-wan-s1_stream}\,s, against
\nm{ttfm-wan-s1_stream_seq}\,s for the same reader sending one request at a time and \nm{ttfm-wan-anndata_zarr_lazy}\,s
for AnnData Zarr (Fig.~\ref{fig:snet}b). The slopes count the round trips paid in sequence: \nm{net-rtts-s1_stream},
\nm{net-rtts-s1_stream_seq} and \nm{net-rtts-anndata_zarr_lazy}. Sustained throughput is
\nm{concurrency-gain-sustained}$\times$ higher with concurrent requests (median of \nm{concurrency-gain-sustained-n}
datasets large enough that each cell is read once).

Latency still lowers the sustained rate. At 1\,Gbit/s the reader delivers \nm{ns-s1_stream-cps-0} cells per second of
\nm{ns-dataset} without added latency and \nm{ns-s1_stream-cps-30} at 30\,ms, against \nm{ns-anndata_zarr_lazy-cps-30}
for AnnData Zarr, \nm{ns-zarr3_shard-cps-30} for sharded Zarr v3 and \nm{ns-soma_ml-cps-30} for TileDB-SOMA ML
(Fig.~\ref{fig:snet}c). The cause is too few bytes in flight: a link of bandwidth $B$ and round trip $T$ is full only with
$B\,T$ bytes outstanding, 3.75\,MB at 1\,Gbit/s and 30\,ms, several minibatches' worth. More requests at once restore the
rate (Fig.~\ref{fig:snet}d): at 30\,ms, eight fill \nm{inf-30-share-8}\% of the link and \nm{inf-30-best-io} fill
\nm{inf-30-best-share}\%, \nm{inf-30-gain} times one request at a time. \texttt{singlet} 1.3.3 therefore sends 32 requests
at once and keeps four minibatches in flight by default; Table~\ref{tab:minibatch} uses the earlier eight and one.

DataLoader workers help loaders that decode in Python but not \sone: scDataset over backed AnnData rises from
\nm{wk-scdataset-local-0} to \nm{wk-scdataset-local-4} cells per second with four workers, while \texttt{S1Cells} goes from
\nm{wk-s1_cells-local-0} to \nm{wk-s1_cells-local-4}, because its decoder already uses every core outside Python's
interpreter lock (Table~\ref{tab:workers}).

\subsection{Random-access costs}

Counted from each format's chunk index over \nm{accN} matrices (Table~\ref{tab:access}), a random minibatch costs \sone{}
a median of \nm{acc-s1-kib}\,KiB in \nm{acc-s1-req} range requests, \nm{acc-s1-overread} times the bytes of the cells
themselves, because blocks start and end inside chunks that are fetched whole. AnnData Zarr fetches
\nm{acc-zarr-kib}\,KiB in \nm{acc-zarr-req} requests and gzip AnnData \nm{acc-h5ad_gzip-kib}\,KiB in
\nm{acc-h5ad_gzip-req}. The chunk size sets the trade-off (Fig.~\ref{fig:snet}e): on \nm{ns-dataset}, chunks of 4,096
non-zeros instead of 49,152 cut the over-read from \nm{chunk-hlca_core-49152-overread} to
\nm{chunk-hlca_core-4096-overread} for a file \nm{chunk-hlca_core-4096-size}\% larger.

\subsection{A real object store}

Read from Cloudflare R2 over the public internet (Table~\ref{tab:r2}), a one-byte range request took a median of
\nm{r2-hlca_core-rtt}\,ms, \nm{r2-hlca_core-edge}\,ms of it the path to the Worker, and one connection read
\nm{r2-hlca_core-gbit1}\,Gbit/s. From HLCA core the first minibatch arrived after \nm{r2-hlca_core-s1_stream-first}\,s
with \sone{} and \nm{r2-hlca_core-anndata_zarr_lazy-first}\,s with AnnData Zarr, against
\nm{r2-hlca_core-anndata_inmem-first}\,s to download the h5ad. Sustained, \sone{} delivered

\nm{r2-hlca_core-s1_stream-gb_epoch}\,GiB, which at S3's list prices costs \$\nm{r2-hlca_core-s1_stream-usd_s3}, against
\$\nm{r2-hlca_core-anndata_zarr_lazy-usd_s3} for AnnData Zarr; R2 charges only for requests.

\subsection{Codec details and stored properties}

The format is specified in the repository (\texttt{docs/format-s1.md}); Fig.~\ref{M-fig:how} outlines it. The prefix of a
matrix (header, a chunk index of 24 bytes per chunk, entropy tables and one key per gene that fixes the gene order) is
enough to plan any read; it is a few kilobytes on small matrices and about half a megabyte at a billion non-zeros, almost
all of it index. Chunks hold consecutive cells, at most 49{,}152 non-zeros by default (a denser cell is a chunk of its
own). Genes are ranked by how many cells express them, in classes of four per doubling. Each non-zero becomes one symbol
for the gap class and whether the count exceeds one, followed by raw bits for the rest of the gap and, for larger counts,
a residual. The symbol's table is selected by the cell's number of non-zeros and its progress down the gene list, and is
counted once per matrix. Thirty-two interleaved rANS states~\cite{giesen2014interleaved} share one 16-bit word stream,
which lets the SIMD kernels decode eight to sixteen symbols per instruction. Every state must end at the encoder's
initial value, which catches most corruption of the coded symbols but not of the raw bits.

Table~\ref{M-tab:props} lists properties beyond size and speed. A sample object is immutable and named by a prefix of its
SHA-256, so a link names exact bytes. A digest of the canonical matrix (values, gene identifiers, barcodes) does not depend
on the encoding, so two files holding the same matrix share it. One object holds several matrices, a cells table and
metadata; derived data are stored as recipes the writer has run and checked; the writer rejects non-integer matrices.
Apart from the round trip that checks every file, these properties were not evaluated, and there is no checksum per
chunk.

\subsection{Distance to the count-model floor}
\label{sec:sfloor}

Along the payload ladder (Fig.~\ref{M-fig:floor}a), 32-bit CSC costs \nm{ladder-L0_csc_i32} bits per non-zero, LEB128
\nm{ladder-L1_leb128}, row gaps with zstd-19 \nm{ladder-L3_gaps_leb_zstd19} and the \sone{} payload
\nm{ladder-L4_s1_payload}; the last step combines gene reordering, context modelling and rANS, which the ladder does not
separate. The best of four cross-validated count models is a median of \nm{s1AboveFloorPct}\% shorter than \sone{}
(range \nm{s1AboveFloorPctLo} to \nm{s1AboveFloorPctHi}\%; negative on both read-count matrices, where \sone{} is shorter;
Fig.~\ref{fig:sfloor}a, Table~\ref{tab:floor}). These code lengths are a reference, not a bound: the models pay nothing for
their parameters and are far simpler than latent-variable models such as scVI or GLM-PCA~\cite{lopez2018deep,townes2019feature}.
Knowing a cell's cluster is worth \nm{sharedBpnMedian} bits per non-zero, and a second general-purpose pass returns
\nm{secondMin}--\nm{secondMax} of the \sone{} size (Fig.~\ref{fig:sfloor}b). For the \nm{samplingN} UMI matrices, the
estimated entropy of which molecules were captured~\cite{townes2019feature,grun2014validation,svensson2020droplet} is a
median of \nm{samplingPctMedian}\% of the payload (range \nm{samplingPctLo}--\nm{samplingPctHi}\%;
Fig.~\ref{fig:sfloor}c); the plug-in profile uses the cell's own counts, so this indicates rather than bounds what a
better model could remove.

\subsection{Ecosystem timings}

On pbmc10k\_v3, \texttt{singlet.load} takes \nm{eco-py-singlet}\,s against \nm{eco-py-read-h5ad}\,s for
\texttt{anndata.read\_h5ad}; in R, \texttt{singlet::load(as = "sce")} takes \nm{eco-r-singlet-sce}\,s against
\nm{eco-r-readh5ad}\,s for \texttt{zellkonverter::readH5AD}, and \texttt{as = "seurat"} \nm{eco-r-singlet-seurat}\,s
against \nm{eco-r-read10x-seurat}\,s for \texttt{CreateSeuratObject(Read10X\_h5(\ldots))} (Table~\ref{tab:eco}). On hlca\_core
the ratios are similar, and larger for the Seurat object. The C++ codec builds without architecture flags and selects its kernel at run time.

\subsection{Beyond single cells}
\label{sec:beyond}

On two out-of-domain count matrices, bulk RNA-seq gene counts and a bag-of-words corpus, \sone{} is
\nm{ext-gtex_bulk-ratio-h5ad_gzip}$\times$ and \nm{ext-nytimes_bow-ratio-h5ad_gzip}$\times$ smaller than gzip AnnData.
Both share what the codec exploits, heavy-tailed rank-frequency laws and many small counts.
