The Gene Expression Omnibus holds sequencing data from more than three billion cells. Models train on millions of cells
streamed from cloud storage, and AI agents fetch and analyze public data themselves; both spend much of their time
decoding count matrices. We measured how few bits counts need and how fast they decode, then built \sone{} to approach
both. A \sone{} dataset is a manifest of content-addressed sample files, each indexed by blocks of cells, so a reader
fetches only needed cells by HTTP range request. Within a block, each cell becomes small gene steps and small counts,
coded by rANS in 32 SIMD lanes. On \nm{nMatrices} public matrices, \sone{} files were always smallest,
\nm{ratioBestOther} times smaller than the best alternative and within a median of \nm{redund-s1}\% of the best of four
cross-validated count models. Read cold on four cores, \sone{} was fastest on \nm{dec-s1-fastest-count} of
\nm{dec-paired-n} matrices; from a cloud object store it delivered a first PyTorch minibatch in under a second and
streamed cells tens of times faster than AnnData Zarr, though per core its decoder is slower than byte-oriented codes.
\sone{} brings single-cell count matrices near the limits of size and speed.
