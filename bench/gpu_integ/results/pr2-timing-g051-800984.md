> **Scope of every rate in this file: per node, 2 GPUs.** The job requested one GPU, but the node is exclusive and the job saw both H100s (`gpus=2` in its log). The runs were unpinned and spread over device 0 and device 1. These are not one-GPU rates. Core-seconds per M read pairs and lock-held seconds per run are per run and do not depend on this. The one-GPU measurement is job 803326 (A/B/B/A) and job 803327 (k by T grid), both with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0`.

Candidate c8849bb2 against b0 (c3a6096c, `gpu-bold/bin/b0`). Exclusive node g051, k=8 in flight, T=7, pbmc1k_v3, 40 runs per point, WSHRINK on in the candidate arms, `IDENT=0`, `ALLOW_NO_EMBED=1`. Order: b0, candidate at 2 slots, candidate at 3 slots with MPS, b0 again. Decision metrics are host core-seconds per M read pairs and lock-held seconds per run, never rate.

**Every arm has runs where the non-host GPU screen fell back to the CPU.** The b0 arm too: 12 of 40 and 11 of 40 runs. The candidate arms: 17 of 40 and 16 of 40. The errors are the same (`CUDA-capable device(s) is/are busy or unavailable`, `resource already mapped`). So the fallback does not come from E20 alone. It exists on b0, which has no E20. By the tier 2 rule any fallback rejects a change, so no arm of this job is clean. The means below drop the fallback runs of each arm (clean runs only, simple mean, no ramp-up trimming).

At 3 slots with MPS it is 7% lower in core-seconds and 3% higher in lock held. The two b0 arms agree within 0.5% in core-seconds and 2% in lock held. The span rate of the two b0 arms differs by 7%, so rate cannot decide anything here. Each candidate arm has fewer clean runs than b0, so the comparison rests on 23 to 29 runs per arm.
