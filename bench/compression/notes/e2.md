# E2: block-adaptive model with canonical-order decode (status: incomplete)

Status: a working, bit-exact coder exists; the measurements the plan asked for were not finished. The E2 agent stopped
on an API rate limit; this note was written afterwards from its files by the coordinating session.
Plan: `plans/E2_block_adaptive_rans.md`.

## What exists

- `kernels/e2_core.h`, `e2_tables.h` (generated by `tools/e2_gen_tables.py`), `e2_model.h`, `e2_codec.h`: the codec
  `e2_b1024`. It codes called cells first and then empties, in ascending row-id order. It uses a per-feature ×
  depth-bucket presence model refit between 1024-column blocks from the decoded data, and no shipped model.
- `harness/e2_bench.cpp` (driver with payload accounting and calibrated-clock timing), `harness/e2_cost.cpp`
  (cost model), `harness/e2_prof.h` (profiling hooks), `tools/e2_proto.py` (Python prototype), `tools/e2_sweep*.sh`.
- `results/e2_smoke.tsv`: 10 runs on three files, every one `matrix_ok = 1`.

## Smoke results (payload bits/nnz; names excluded)

| file | e2_b1024 payload | called cells | empties | b_rans16 (Track B) | today's chunks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pbmc_v2_typical exon | 5.84 | 5.70 | 7.45 | 5.74 | 6.85 |
| brain_sn_v3_deep exon | 8.18 | 6.68 | 9.66 | 8.06 | 9.44 |
| tumor_3p exon | 10.58-10.78 | 9.86-9.90 | 10.6-11.0 | 9.87 | 11.91 |

Size: on par with the best measured codecs on the two large files, worse on the tiny one, where the first block's
flat prior is expensive. Speed: about 1,100-1,200 cycles per nonzero on the payload, about 6 Mnnz/s at a 3 GHz clock.
The between-block refit costs a further 10-20% of decode time. The 100-300 Mnnz/s target was not approached. The
decoder is the scalar reference with an exact survival-table search; no interleaving or SIMD was written.

## What the partial result still says

Coding in ascending row-id order with block-refit tables costs nothing in size against the frequency-rank-order
static coder (5.84 vs 5.74, 8.18 vs 8.06 bits/nnz on the large files). That removes the need for a per-file row
permutation and for the post-decode sort that halves Track B's speed, so the speed problem reduces to writing a fast
decoder for this layout. Track B's 32-lane rANS and Idea 1's SIMD tree search are the parts to reuse.
